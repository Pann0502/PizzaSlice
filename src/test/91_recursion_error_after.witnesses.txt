  - Subtlety: to REACH the target, foo() must terminate, i.e. the recursion must
    bottom out at B1=F. Under no-unroll we do not pin that; we assume termination
    on the path to the target. If your semantics require an explicit "recursion
    returns" marker, that is the one extra waypoint to consider.
  - Good contrast with 90: same recursion shape, but the error's placement moves
    it from "inside the recursion" to "after it".
